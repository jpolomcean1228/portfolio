MyNYL payments redesign

Capgemini, client New York Life — 2026 to present

A sixteen-feature program increment across five sprints and two scrum teams, with money movement in scope and committed release dates that held.

I own discovery, requirements, backlog and prioritization on New York Life's policyholder self-service platform, and lead the payments redesign end to end — mode-change conversion, one-time payment redesign, and refreshed interface standards across one-time payment, loan repayment, and universal and term life products, sequenced across three consecutive sprints. Weekly reporting publishes red-amber-green health on scope, schedule and resources alongside epic-level development and QA completion, so gaps surface mid-sprint instead of at release cutover.

Alongside delivery I drove eligibility rule reductions on payments and surrenders, cutting policyholder-facing restrictions on transactions that previously routed to manual handling.

Agentic benefits content

JPMorgan Chase — Employee Experience Platforms

The right content, for the right person, in their own geo-locale — in three clicks or less.

The problem wasn't that employees couldn't find benefits information. It was that finding it meant a search box, a results page, a PDF, and a guess about whether the policy applied to their country and their plan. The answer was to structure benefits content at the source with tags an agent could reason over, so the system could resolve a question in context and return the version that actually applies to the person asking, scoped to their location.

I pressure-tested the target outcomes and helped ship it — the design credit belongs to the team. What I owned was whether the outcome we were aiming at was the right one and whether the thing we shipped hit it: three clicks as a hard ceiling, geo-locale correctness as a pass condition, not a nice-to-have.

  • Tag-based content model
  • Agentic retrieval
  • Geo-locale scoping
  • Frictionless search

Extraction and retrieval for Medicare compliance

Insight Ally — ADR Connect and R2 Case Management

An extraction pipeline and a grounded regulatory knowledge base, both gated on evaluation rather than vibes.

The extraction side reads Medicare Additional Documentation Request letters across 20+ structured fields. Before release I wrote adversarial test documents with deliberate traps and a companion answer key, because the interesting question isn't whether a pipeline works on clean input — it's what it does when a date is ambiguous or a field is missing, and whether it says so or quietly invents one.

The retrieval side turns dense Skilled Nursing Facility Medicare regulation into a queryable reference with a question bank behind it, cutting reviewer research time per case. Alongside both, automation specifications for the document pull itself: PointClickCare in Playwright, NetHealth in Power Automate Desktop.

  • LLM extraction, 20+ fields
  • Adversarial test generation
  • RAG over regulation
  • LLM-as-judge evaluation
